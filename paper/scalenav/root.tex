\begin{abstract}
A reactive flight policy cannot remember a detour around a long obstacle or
use semantic evidence beyond valid depth.  ScaleNav adds route-level memory to
a neural local planner through a bounded graph of collision-checked free-space
bubbles and witness paths.  Depth-clipped open-vocabulary observations become
persistent far-field hypotheses whose continuous risk fields rank nearby
witnesses without changing geometric validity.  The selected world-frame
witness is remapped after asynchronous graph updates and converted into an
ordered corridor for dynamically feasible local execution.  ScaleNav therefore
adds long-horizon, language-conditioned route preference without an occupancy
grid or ESDF.  On an independent 1,800-route offline benchmark, corridor
conditioning reduces collision from $7.78\%$ to $0.33\%$ and corridor violation
from $54.33\%$ to $8.67\%$, while progress changes from $6.117$ to $4.230$~m.
\end{abstract}

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\section{INTRODUCTION}
Unknown-environment flight couples fast local avoidance with a slower route
decision.  One-stage neural planners map depth, motion, and a relative goal to
dynamically feasible trajectories \cite{yopo}, but their sensing horizon cannot
resolve an early left--right choice around a long obstacle.  RGB may also
recognize risk beyond valid depth, yet a reactive policy has nowhere to retain
that evidence.

ScaleNav resolves both mismatches.  Depth builds a persistent graph of
free-space bubbles and collision-checked witness paths.  A depth-clipped
semantic ray creates a far-field node at fixed optical depth, and its
continuous field changes witness cost before the risk enters depth range.  The
planner commits a world-frame witness, remaps it after asynchronous graph
replacement, and supplies an ordered safe corridor to the local policy.
Semantics only ranks collision-valid alternatives: it can select a poor route
but cannot certify occupied space as free.

The main contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
  \setlength\itemsep{0pt}
  \item \textbf{Semantic foresight on free-space topology.}
  Continuous open-vocabulary risk over collision-checked witnesses enables
  early branch selection without changing validity.
  \item \textbf{Persistent far-field semantic nodes.}
  Depth-clipped observations persist as bounded hypotheses and are promoted
  when measured bubbles reach them.
  \item \textbf{Route identity across graph replacement.}
  A world-frame witness and hysteretic gate preserve the route across
  asynchronous updates without stale pointers.
  \item \textbf{Corridor-conditioned local execution.}
  The accepted witness conditions dynamics-aware primitives without an
  occupancy grid or ESDF.
\end{itemize}

The evaluation separates system integration, local route adherence, and graph
persistence.  All reported semantic observations use PEARL.

\section{RELATED WORK}
Reactive planners trade global consistency for latency: RAPPIDS queries a
depth image \cite{rappids}, EGO-Planner avoids ESDF construction
\cite{egoplanner}, and YOPO directly generates motion \cite{yopo}.  FASTER
combines safe search with trajectory optimization \cite{faster}; SUPER advances
this line with safety-assured point-cloud corridors above $20$~m/s
\cite{super}.
ScaleNav does not reproduce SUPER's guarantee or speed.  It adds the orthogonal
interface of text-conditioned route memory above a local executor.

Persistent maps include hierarchical roadmaps \cite{tare}, distance fields
\cite{voxblox}, and receding or clustered free-space corridors
\cite{bubble,epic}.  Their costs remain geometric.  ScaleNav instead preserves
a selected witness across graph replacement and attaches open-vocabulary risk
without changing collision validity.

Open-vocabulary models align text with image regions \cite{clip,pearl} and can
be lifted into 3-D maps \cite{openscene}.  VLFM selects
semantic frontiers, RayFronts retains beyond-range semantic rays, and WildOS
scores sparse graph frontiers for object search \cite{vlfm,rayfronts,wildos}.
ScaleNav addresses avoidance: risk repels a route continuously along its
witness, including before depth reaches the observation.

\section{PROBLEM FORMULATION}
Let the vehicle state at time $t$ be
$\vect{x}_t=(\vect{p}_t,\vect{v}_t,\vect{a}_t,\vect{R}_t)$.
The sensors provide an RGB image $I_t$, a depth image $D_t$, and
odometry.
The mission gives a world-frame goal $\vect{g}$ and a text query $q$ that
describes regions to avoid.
No prior geometric map is available.

The planner must output a dynamically feasible local trajectory $\tau_t$.
We separate this problem into a slow route decision and a fast motion
decision:
\begin{align}
  (\mathcal{W}_t,\vect{g}^{\mathrm{front}}_t,
   \vect{g}^{\mathrm{loc}}_t)
    &= \Pi_{\mathrm{topo}}(\mathcal{G}_t,I_{0:t},D_{0:t},q,\vect{g}), \\
  \tau_t
    &= \Pi_{\mathrm{local}}(D_t,\vect{v}_t,\vect{a}_t,
       \vect{R}_t^\top(\vect{g}^{\mathrm{front}}_t-\vect{p}_t),
       \mathcal{B}_t).
\end{align}
Here $\mathcal{W}_t$ is the accepted world-frame witness and
$\mathcal{B}_t$ is its fixed-size corridor representation.  The local policy
is a corridor-conditioned local policy.  It receives one $160\times96$ depth channel,
body-frame motion, the frontier goal, and the ordered corridor:
\begin{equation}
 \vect{o}_t = [\vect{v}^{B}_t,\vect{a}^{B}_t,
                (\vect{g}^{\mathrm{front}}_t-\vect{p}_t)^{B}] \in \R^9,
 \qquad
 \mathcal{B}_t\in\R^{12\times4}.
\end{equation}
The $12$ rows contain bubble center and safe radius; a binary route mask marks
valid rows.  The local policy does not create a new frontier or change the global
homotopy.  It chooses a dynamically feasible primitive inside the accepted
route.

The primitive lattice follows the released YOPO parameterization.  The
$160\times96$ depth feature map is decoded on a $3\times5$ grid, giving
$N_{\mathrm{prim}}=15$ candidate directions.  Each candidate predicts a
nine-dimensional terminal state $[\vect{p}_1,\vect{v}_1,\vect{a}_1]\in\R^9$
and one scalar score.  The terminal state is converted to a smooth
polynomial primitive using the fixed-duration boundary-value solver: the
current position, velocity, and acceleration are fixed at the start, while
the predicted terminal derivatives are free.  Candidate selection is thus a
discrete choice over dynamically parameterized primitives, rather than direct
regression of a timed expert path.

For the route-aware variant, each corridor row contributes the body-frame
center, its safe radius, and a validity bit.  The network concatenates the
validity bit with the four geometric values, so the learned route feature has
$12\times5$ scalars even though the geometric corridor itself is
$12\times4$.  Rows beyond the available witness are zero-padded and masked.
When the route contract is invalid or stale, all rows are masked and the
frontier-only YOPO input is recovered.  This explicit zero-input behavior
keeps route conditioning from becoming a hidden safety dependency.

We use a fixed-height planning layer in the current experiments to isolate the
horizontal branch-selection problem.
The depth sensing, point memory, and local trajectory execution remain 3-D.
The proposed semantic cost is defined on 3-D witness polylines and does not
require the fixed-height assumption.

\section{PERSISTENT FREE-SPACE TOPOLOGY}
\label{sec:topology}
\subsection{Bounded Geometric Memory}
Depth is back-projected into obstacle points and free-ray evidence.
The implementation voxel-filters the current frame and retains at most
$N_{\max}$ points; no points from earlier frames are kept in this buffer.
It does not construct occupancy voxels or an ESDF.

Following prior bubble-graph construction \cite{epic}, collision-free bubbles
are clustered into graph vertices.
A vertex is
\begin{equation}
 v_i=(\vect{c}_i,\rho_i,z_i,h_i),
\end{equation}
where $\vect{c}_i$ is its center, $\rho_i$ is free-space clearance, $z_i$ is
semantic state, and $h_i$ is a persistent identity record.
An edge stores a collision-checked witness polyline
\begin{equation}
 \pi_{ij}=\{\vect{w}^{ij}_0,\ldots,\vect{w}^{ij}_{M_{ij}}\},
 \quad
 L_{ij}=\sum_{m=1}^{M_{ij}}
 \|\vect{w}^{ij}_m-\vect{w}^{ij}_{m-1}\|_2.
\end{equation}
The witness is important twice.
It is the executable geometric connection, and it is the support on which
semantic distance is measured.
The chord $\vect{c}_j-\vect{c}_i$ is insufficient because a valid witness may
bend around an obstacle.

Geometry is updated in a background graph.
After local bubble generation, region clustering, and graph connection, an
atomic swap exposes the new graph to planning.
The point budget and spatial radius bound memory.
Transiently missing bubbles receive a short grace period before deletion.

\subsection{Clearance-Aware Edge Cost}
Let $\rho_{ij}=\min(\rho_i,\rho_j)$ and let $\rho^\star$ be the target
clearance.
The implemented soft clearance term is
\begin{equation}
 C_{\mathrm{clr}}(e_{ij})=
 \lambda_{\mathrm{clr}}L_{ij}
 \left[
 \max\left(0,\frac{\rho^\star-\rho_{ij}}{\rho^\star}\right)
 \right]^2.
\label{eq:clearance}
\end{equation}
It prefers open routes but does not declare a narrow valid edge invalid.
Geometric feasibility remains determined by the bubble graph and its witness
checks.

\section{SEMANTIC FORESIGHT}
\label{sec:semantics}
\subsection{Semantic Observation Model}
The frozen PEARL model maps $(I_t,q)$ to a patch heatmap.
The heatmap is max-pooled on a $5\times3$ grid, and a low-quantile
background baseline is subtracted so that weak whole-image responses do not
become risk.
Every retained patch is projected by the calibrated pinhole model at a fixed
optical-axis depth $\ell_s$, independent of the measured depth:
\begin{equation}
 \vect{y}_{u,t}=\vect{p}_t+\mat{R}_t
 \left(\vect{t}_{\mathrm{cam}}+\ell_s\widetilde{\vect{d}}_u\right),
\label{eq:projection}
\end{equation}
where $\vect{p}_t$ and $\mat{R}_t$ are the body pose,
$\vect{t}_{\mathrm{cam}}$ is the camera translation, and
$\widetilde{\vect{d}}_u=[1,x_u,y_u]^\top$ is the unnormalized pinhole ray.
Thus every endpoint has the same optical-axis depth, while off-axis endpoints
have a larger Euclidean range. Measured depth is not consulted by this
projection and therefore cannot suppress semantic evidence.
For a graph node $v_i$, the associated observation is
\begin{equation}
 \hat{s}_{i,t}=
 \max_{u:\|\vect{y}_{u,t}-\vect{c}_i\|\le r_a}
 s_{u,t}
 \exp\left(-\frac{\|\vect{y}_{u,t}-\vect{c}_i\|^2}{2\sigma_a^2}\right).
\label{eq:association}
\end{equation}
An exponential moving average removes single-frame score jumps:
\begin{equation}
 s_{i,t}=(1-\alpha)s_{i,t-1}+\alpha\hat{s}_{i,t}.
\label{eq:ema}
\end{equation}
The tuple $(s_i,c_i)$ denotes the stored score and confidence.
It is restored by persistent node identity after graph replacement.
Four evidence factors---multi-row consistency, FOV margin, suspected ground
response, and observation freshness---modulate the confidence instead of
rejecting observations outright.

\subsection{Far-Field Semantic Nodes}
Each projected point $\vect{y}_{u,t}$ becomes a persistent graph node with
unknown geometry state and a semantic score.
Repeat observations within $r_m$ update the same node through
(\ref{eq:ema}), and overlapping fields of view across frames re-use the same
persistent identity through ray association.
The node set is bounded by a maximum count, a minimum score, and a minimum
mutual separation, and nodes do not expire with time.
Semantic nodes are stored with the global graph and survive the sliding
geometric window; risk queries touch only nodes inside the local planning
radius plus an influence band.
Each node is connected through the same local graph machinery as geometric
nodes.
When measured geometry later grows a bubble at the node's position, the node
is promoted from unknown to verified while its semantic identity is
retained.

A far-field semantic node is not a hard obstacle.
It is a hypothesis about route risk beyond valid depth.
This distinction lets low-risk clipped rays remain useful as forward graph
hypotheses while high-risk rays repel the selected route.

\subsection{Continuous Risk on Witness Paths}
Endpoint-only node costs react too late.
A risky semantic node may sit beyond a fork while neither endpoint of the
fork has a high semantic score.
We therefore measure every semantic node against the complete witness
polyline:
\begin{equation}
 d_{k,ij}=\min_{\vect{x}\in\pi_{ij}}
             \|\vect{y}_k-\vect{x}\|_2.
\label{eq:anchor_distance}
\end{equation}
The measured endpoint risk is
\begin{equation}
 r^{\mathrm{node}}_{ij}=\frac{1}{2}(s_i c_i+s_j c_j).
\end{equation}
Semantic node $k$ induces the edge risk
\begin{equation}
 r^{\mathrm{ff}}_{k,ij}=s_kc_k
 \exp\left(-\frac{d_{k,ij}^2}{2(R_s/2)^2}\right),
 \qquad d_{k,ij}\le R_s.
\label{eq:field}
\end{equation}
The implementation ties the Gaussian bandwidth to its finite support,
$\sigma_s=R_s/2$, rather than exposing two independently tuned spatial
scales.
The combined risk uses a maximum rather than a sum:
\begin{equation}
 r_{ij}=\max\left(r^{\mathrm{node}}_{ij},
                  \max_k r^{\mathrm{ff}}_{k,ij}\right).
\label{eq:risk}
\end{equation}
This avoids making risk depend on the arbitrary density of nearby semantic
nodes.
The semantic edge penalty is the barrier
\begin{equation}
 C_{\mathrm{sem}}(e_{ij})=
 \lambda_{\mathrm{sem}}L_{ij}[-\log(\max(\epsilon,1-r_{ij}))],
 \qquad \epsilon=10^{-3}.
\label{eq:semantic_cost}
\end{equation}
It is nearly linear at low risk and grows sharply near unit risk, while the
clamp keeps the planner finite for saturated observations.
The factor $L_{ij}$ makes exposure accumulate with travel distance.

\section{RISK-AWARE ROLLING SEARCH}
\subsection{Edge and Path Objectives}
For an edge $e_{ij}$, the geometric traversal cost stored by the graph is
$W_{ij}$.  The deployed configuration does not discount remembered edges
during a fresh search; route persistence is enforced by the accepted-route
gate described below.  The complete candidate edge cost is
\begin{equation}
 C(e_{ij})=W_{ij}+C_{\mathrm{clr}}(e_{ij})+C_{\mathrm{sem}}(e_{ij}).
\label{eq:edge_cost}
\end{equation}
For a graph path $P$, we separately accumulate its geometric and risk terms:
\begin{align}
 G(P)&=\sum_{e\in P}W_e,\\
 R(P)&=\sum_{e\in P}
       \left[C_{\mathrm{clr}}(e)+C_{\mathrm{sem}}(e)\right].
\end{align}
Keeping them separate is important: terminal selection may weight geometry
and accumulated risk differently, while neither term receives a hidden
remembered-edge discount.

The slow layer maintains three goal levels: the \emph{mission goal}
$\vect{g}$, a route-level \emph{frontier goal} selected inside the local
graph window of radius $R_g$, and a control-level \emph{local goal} on the
accepted witness.
The mission goal may lie outside the currently connected graph.
We therefore search all reachable non-viewpoint vertices within radius
$R_g$.
Terminal selection is a single soft loss: only disconnection, collision,
insufficient clearance, or a missing witness is a hard rejection.
For candidate $v$, let $d_{\mathrm{rsv}}(v)$ be the reserve shortage toward
the mission goal, $d_{\mathrm{dir}}(v)$ the misalignment with the mission
direction, $d_{\mathrm{fov}}(v)$ the offset from the field of view, and
$d_{\mathrm{sm}}(v)$ the heading discontinuity at the vehicle.
The implemented frontier-goal score is
\begin{align}
 J(v)={}&\alpha_gG(P_v)+R(P_v)
       +w_{\mathrm{rsv}}d_{\mathrm{rsv}}(v)+w_{\mathrm{dir}}d_{\mathrm{dir}}(v)
       \nonumber\\
       &+w_{\mathrm{fov}}d_{\mathrm{fov}}(v)
       +w_{\mathrm{sm}}d_{\mathrm{sm}}(v).
\label{eq:terminal}
\end{align}
Direction, FOV, and reserve terms guide the search but never disqualify a
feasible terminal; once the mission goal enters the local window, a bubble
search validates the final connection within a short range.
Graph A* uses $f=g+h$ with millisecond-scale connection budgets.
The best recovered path ends at
\begin{equation}
 v_t^\star=\arg\min_{v\in\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{cand}}}J(v),
\end{equation}
and its terminal becomes the frontier goal.

\subsection{Route Identity Across Graph Updates}
Incremental graph replacement can change node objects even when the physical
route is unchanged.
ScaleNav therefore stores the selected witness in world coordinates:
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal{W}_{t-1}=\{\vect{w}_0,\ldots,\vect{w}_K\}.
\end{equation}
The vehicle is projected to this polyline and only a forward window of length
$H_r$ is retained:
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal{W}^{+}_{t-1}=
 \operatorname{crop}(\mathcal{W}_{t-1},
                     \operatorname{proj}(\vect{p}_t,\mathcal{W}_{t-1}),H_r).
\end{equation}
An edge in the new graph is remembered if its geometry follows this window
within a lateral tolerance.
The incumbent terminal is restored by persistent node identity first, with
nearest-neighbor remapping as a fallback.
Equation (\ref{eq:edge_cost}) ranks a fresh candidate without a historical
edge discount; persistence enters only when the route gate compares that
candidate with the remapped incumbent.

A challenger replaces the incumbent only through hysteresis: its risk must
improve by more than a margin, or its total cost must drop below a fixed
ratio of the incumbent cost.
A high-risk incumbent is latched until its route risk falls back below a
release threshold, and prefix-preserving route extensions are exempt from
the switch test.
The prior is released when the vehicle leaves the route or when semantic
risk along the route changes by more than $\Delta_r$:
\begin{equation}
 \left|\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{W}_{t-1};t)-
        \mathcal{R}(\mathcal{W}_{t-1};t-1)\right|>\Delta_r.
\end{equation}
This balance is central to the design.
Route memory prevents left-right oscillation.
Risk-triggered release lets new language evidence change the homotopy.

\subsubsection{Asynchronous Contract and Complexity}
The geometry, semantic, and control streams run on separate clocks.  Every
RGB, depth, odometry, graph, and semantic record carries a source timestamp;
the planner consumes a consistent snapshot rather than joining callbacks by
arrival order.  A graph worker builds topology and witnesses from the latest
bounded depth buffer, then publishes them through an atomic replacement.  A
planning tick therefore sees either the previous complete graph or the next
complete graph, never a partially rebuilt set of vertices and edges.

At each tick, semantic state is read together with the graph snapshot and the
remembered world-frame witness.  Timestamp and freshness gates reject stale
state, while a valid incumbent remains eligible during a short graph grace
period.  The route gate can consequently preserve a physical corridor across
replacement without retaining pointers into the old graph.  If the incumbent
fails its geometric witness check, the planner removes the route prior before
searching again; semantic state can rank a replacement but cannot make the
failed witness executable.

Let $V$ and $E$ denote the vertices and candidate edges in the local graph,
$M$ the retained semantic nodes, and $Q$ the number of samples used to measure
each witness.  A naive semantic edge evaluation is $O(EQM)$, but the planner
first restricts nodes to the edge influence radius, so the practical work is
proportional to the local semantic neighborhood rather than the full history.
The bounded point buffer, graph window, and semantic-node cap give memory
growth $O(N_{\max}+V+E+M)$ independent of flight duration.  These bounds keep
the slow route layer from competing with the fixed-rate local controller.

\subsection{Corridor-Conditioned Local Policy}
A frontier goal communicates where to move but not which homotopy to preserve;
an endpoint-conditioned policy can therefore shortcut away from the accepted
witness.  ScaleNav publishes a 10-m local goal and conditions the policy on an
ordered corridor extracted from the same witness.

The witness is refined with fixed endpoints and protected turns; an adjustment
is accepted only when every segment stays safe and bottleneck clearance
improves.  We then sample ordered bubbles
\begin{equation}
 b_k=(\vect{c}_k,r_k),\quad
 r_k=\max\left(0,d(\vect{c}_k)-r_{\mathrm{rob}}-r_{\mathrm{safe}}\right),
\label{eq:bubble}
\end{equation}
where $d(\vect{c}_k)$ is the obstacle distance at center $\vect{c}_k$.
The policy receives depth, body velocity and acceleration, the frontier goal, and a
fixed-$K$ ordered bubble list with a route mask:
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal{B}_t=\{([\vect{c}_k^{B}]_t,r_k,m_k)\}_{k=1}^{K},
 \qquad K=12,
\label{eq:route_input}
\end{equation}
where $m_k\in\{0,1\}$ is the route mask.  The contract also carries route
identity, timestamp, frame, remaining length, quality, and validity.  Invalid
routes mask all bubbles and recover the frontier-only input.
Figure~\ref{fig:corridor_policy} summarizes the resulting local-policy
interface and primitive selection.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{pics/corridor_conditioned_yopo.pdf}
\caption{Corridor-conditioned local execution.  The accepted witness is
refined into $K=12$ ordered clearance bubbles.  Depth, 9-D state,
\texttt{frontier\_goal}, and the masked corridor enter the unchanged YOPO
trunk, which scores 15 dynamically parameterized primitives.  The corridor
loss penalizes primitive points outside the selected bubbles while preserving
the local policy's freedom to slow down or bend around newly observed
obstacles.}
\label{fig:corridor_policy}
\end{figure}

The network and trajectory generator remain unchanged; the score adds a route
term over sampled primitive points:
\begin{equation}
 L_{\mathrm{cor}}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}
 \min_k\max\left(0,\|\vect{p}_n-\vect{c}_k\|_2-r_k\right)^2.
\label{eq:corridor_loss}
\end{equation}
The witness is geometric, not a timed expert trajectory: the graph selects the
corridor and the policy retains all 15 primitives within it.  Training keeps
YOPO's original terms and adds (\ref{eq:corridor_loss}) over 30 samples, with
extra weight on the maximum violation.  No behavior cloning is used.

\section{EXPERIMENTS}
\label{sec:experiments}
We organize the evaluation into three blocks.  The \emph{closed-loop system
evaluation} compares ScaleNav with YOPO, EGO-Planner, and SUPER, varies the
open-vocabulary query, removes individual ScaleNav components, and profiles
runtime and memory under one paired protocol.  The \emph{local-policy
evaluation} isolates corridor conditioning on an independent 1,800-route
offline benchmark.  The \emph{persistence and semantic-exposure analysis}
combines semantic-cost replay with an outbound/return experiment that tests
graph reuse and measures the spatial exposure of both legs.

This decomposition separates the three intended sources of behavior: route
memory is supplied by the graph, local feasibility by the corridor constraint,
and route preference by open-vocabulary evidence.

The closed-loop evaluation and persistence analysis use the UE Map2 scene
through Colosseum/AirSim \cite{airsim}, synchronized RGB-D and odometry.  The
one-way closed-loop conditions use the fixed mission goal
$(x,y,z)=(0.0,140.0,1.6)$~m; each trial is a single one-way flight and no
return leg is included in its time, distance, or success metrics.  The offline
and graph-reuse protocols are specified separately in their subsections.
The topological planner operates on a 1.6-m horizontal graph layer.
Each closed-loop condition contains at
least 20 paired trials with identical initial states, goals, scenes, and random
seeds across methods.  We report the mean, standard deviation, and 95\%
confidence interval.
Paired continuous metrics use a two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank test.
Success proportions use paired bootstrap intervals.
Failures remain in safety and success metrics and are not silently removed
from path-efficiency statistics.

\subsection{Closed-Loop System Evaluation}
\subsubsection{Methods and Conditions}
The closed-loop external comparison includes the following published planners:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{YOPO}: the original local policy with the frontier goal and no
  persistent route constraint.
  \item \textbf{EGO-Planner}: an ESDF-free geometric local planner
  \cite{egoplanner}.
  \item \textbf{SUPER}: a safety-assured high-speed planner using dual
  trajectories and point-cloud corridors \cite{super}.
  \item \textbf{ScaleNav}: persistent topology, semantic foresight, route
  memory, and corridor-conditioned local execution.
\end{itemize}

For the language-condition comparison, the planner and scene geometry are held fixed while only the text
condition changes: no query, known-object queries such as ``avoid buildings''

The closed-loop benchmark covers isolated trees, dense forests, long walls,

unseen power-line scene.  The component ablation uses matched closed-loop
scenes with one ScaleNav component removed at a time.

\subsubsection{Metrics}
Following the benchmark conventions of EGO-Planner and SUPER, the primary
closed-loop metrics are success rate (goal reached without collision or
timeout), safe rate (no collision or dynamically infeasible trajectory),
collision/failure rate, path length,
flight time, average flight speed, and planning time.  These metrics are
reported for every external baseline and ScaleNav under identical starts and
goals.  For shortest mission length $L^\star$, flown length $L$, and binary
success $S$, we report the all-trial path efficiency
$\eta_{\rm SPL}=S L^\star/\max(L,L^\star)$.  Completion time $T$ runs from
the goal command until the vehicle enters the goal tolerance with admissible
speed, and $\bar v=L/T$.  Raw $L$, $T$, and $\bar v$ are reported over
successful trials; failures remain represented by success, collision,
$\eta_{\rm SPL}$, and the fixed timeout.  This prevents early failures from
appearing artificially short or fast.  Minimum clearance at the executed
vehicle position is retained as a secondary safety diagnostic.  Clearance of
the ScaleNav global witness is reported only as a guide-quality diagnostic:
YOPO performs local obstacle avoidance around that guide, so witness clearance
is neither executed-flight clearance nor collision ground truth.

The language comparison additionally reports the change in selected route,
query compliance, decision distance, route-switch count, and semantic exposure
$E_{\mathrm{sem}}=\frac{1}{L}\int_0^T r(\vect{p}(t))v(t)\,dt$.  System profiling reports PEARL
latency, graph-update wall time, rolling-A* latency, graph size, resident
memory, local-policy rate, and dropped semantic frames.  Region precision,
recall, task compliance, and stale-response rejection are reported only for
the open-vocabulary front end.

\subsection{Offline Local-Policy Evaluation}
\textit{Protocol.} The corridor-conditioned checkpoint is trained on $2{,}250$ routes and
evaluated on an independent $1{,}800$-route benchmark containing cylindrical

\subsection{Persistence and Semantic-Exposure Analysis}
\textit{Protocol.} This exploratory mechanism analysis keeps the graph process
alive while the vehicle flies from $(0,0,1.6)$~m to $(0,140,1.6)$~m and back;
neither graph nor semantic memory is cleared at the turn.  Each leg begins at
the corresponding new-goal event and ends at mission completion.  We verify
reuse from the goal-state flag, absence of background initialization, nonzero
retained nodes, and newly inserted nodes per rebuild.  Route-switch events
measure stability; planner-update and background-rebuild statistics measure
online cost.  Flight time is measured between mission events, and path length
is integrated from synchronized 100-Hz odometry.

For spatial exposure, point clouds are transformed into the world frame,
cropped to $z\in[0.3,3.2]$~m, and merged into 0.5-m occupied cells.  Local
obstacle density is the occupied fraction within 3~m of each trajectory
sample.  Final high-risk semantic nodes are restricted to a 4-m vertical band
around the flight layer.  We report mean and P90 obstacle density, minimum
semantic-node distance, and trajectory fraction within 5~m of a high-risk
node.

The causal follow-up replays identical graph snapshots and observations over
$\lambda_{\rm sem}\in\{1,2,4\}$ and $R_s\in\{3,5,7\}$~m, with
$\sigma_s=R_s/2$ and $(2,5)$ as the deployed setting.  It reports branch
agreement, query compliance, semantic exposure, path length, and route-switch
count without changing geometry or collision checks.  In particular, the
paired $\lambda_{\rm sem}=2$ versus $0$ replay is required to separate semantic
preference from geometric sparsity.

\textit{Observed behavior.} The current analysis contains two complete round trips, rather
than the ten specified for the final efficiency claim.  Both return legs reused the
outbound graph without background reinitialization.  Newly inserted nodes per
rebuild fell by $62.7\%$ and $61.5\%$, while route switches fell from 18 to 12
and from 15 to 10.  This confirms graph persistence, but not an end-to-end
speedup: rebuild mean increased by $21.5\%$ and $17.6\%$, and rebuild P95 by
$15.0\%$ and $31.3\%$, as the retained graph and semantic working set grew.
Return flight time changed by $-8.8\%$ and $-1.3\%$, whereas path length
increased by $1.3\%$ and $4.6\%$.

The spatial analysis of the second round trip shows that return mean and P90
obstacle density decreased by $8.2\%$ and $28.6\%$.  The minimum distance to a final
high-risk semantic node increased from 2.56 to 6.93~m, and the trajectory
fraction within 5~m of such nodes decreased from $8.7\%$ to zero.  Thus the
return selected a longer corridor through less clutter and farther from the
final semantic-risk set.  This is correlation rather than semantic causation:
the two legs have opposite viewpoints, the semantic set accumulates during the
flight, and lower geometric density favors the same detour.  The paired
semantic-on/off replay defined above remains necessary.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\section{DISCUSSION AND LIMITATIONS}
ScaleNav adds route-scale memory and semantic branch guidance around an
existing local planner.  Route conditioning preserves local dynamic-obstacle
response, while semantics remains a preference rather than a safety certificate.
Far-field nodes are hypotheses; geometric checks remain authoritative.

The semantic parameters have distinct and monotone roles.  Increasing
$\lambda_{\rm sem}$ strengthens preference for lower-risk routes without
changing their geometric validity, whereas increasing $R_s$ expands the
region influenced by each observation and simultaneously broadens its tied
Gaussian bandwidth.  Excessive values can therefore produce conservative
detours, while small values postpone the branch decision.  The fixed
sensitivity protocol above is needed to establish how wide the stable
operating range is; until those replay results are populated, robustness to
these parameters should not be inferred from the nominal setting alone.

SUPER and ScaleNav address different layers: SUPER provides geometric,
safety-assured high-speed execution, whereas ScaleNav provides persistent,
language-conditioned route choice.  Their composition is not evaluated here.

The current topological search uses a fixed-height layer, and the offline
benchmark uses zero initial motion rather than the online C++ M4 search.  A
fully 3-D closed-loop evaluation is future work.  The semantic interface is
model-independent: boxes, points, or masks can be rasterized into the same
heatmap contract, but no alternative model is evaluated here.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\section{CONCLUSION}
ScaleNav adds semantic foresight at the topological route layer.
Depth-clipped open-vocabulary observations become persistent far-field
semantic nodes whose continuous field acts on collision-free witness paths and
can change a branch before the risk is geometrically observed.
A world-frame witness memory preserves route identity across asynchronous graph
replacement.  The corridor-conditioned policy receives depth, state, frontier goal,
and ordered witness corridor bubbles, while the topological layer publishes the
local goal for execution.
This yields a clean separation: topology remembers, semantics ranks, and the
local policy flies inside the selected corridor.
